# Plain Language Summary: Semaglutide for People with Overweight or Obesity and Heart Disease (SELECT Trial)

## What was this trial about?

This trial tested whether a medicine called semaglutide (given as a weekly injection under the skin) could reduce the chance of serious heart problems in people who were overweight or living with obesity, had existing heart or blood vessel disease, and did not have diabetes. Participants were required to be 45 years or older and have a body-mass index of 27 or higher. People with diabetes, severe heart failure, kidney failure needing dialysis, or a recent heart or stroke event were not included.

Semaglutide was compared against a placebo (a dummy injection that looked the same but contained no active medicine). Neither participants nor their doctors knew who was receiving which treatment.

In total, 17,604 people took part: 8,803 received semaglutide and 8,801 received placebo. The average age was about 62 years, and around 72% were men. The trial was conducted at 804 sites in 41 countries. On average, participants were followed for a little over three years.

## Main result: major heart problems

The main question the trial was designed to answer was whether semaglutide reduced the combined occurrence of death from heart-related causes, non-fatal heart attack, or non-fatal stroke — collectively called a "major adverse cardiovascular event."

This event occurred in 569 of 8,803 people (6.5%) taking semaglutide, compared with 701 of 8,801 people (8.0%) taking placebo. Fewer people receiving semaglutide experienced this combined outcome than those receiving placebo, and this difference was considered statistically reliable — that is, unlikely to be due to chance.

## Additional outcomes examined in a fixed order

The trial's analysis plan specified that certain additional ("secondary") outcomes would only be formally tested one after another, in a pre-set order, and only if the outcome before it in the sequence showed a reliable result. If an outcome in the sequence failed to reach the required threshold, testing of all later outcomes in the sequence would stop, and those later outcomes could not be treated as formally confirmed findings.

**First in the sequence: death from cardiovascular causes.** This occurred in 2.5% of people taking semaglutide compared with 3.0% of people taking placebo. This difference did not reach the threshold set in the analysis plan for being counted as a reliable finding. As specified in the analysis plan, this meant that the testing sequence stopped at this point.

**Later outcomes in the sequence: the combined heart failure outcome (death from cardiovascular causes, or hospitalisation/urgent visit for heart failure) and death from any cause.** Because the outcome before them in the sequence did not reach the required threshold, these two outcomes were not formally tested, in line with the pre-specified analysis plan. This means that no confirmed conclusion about a benefit for these outcomes can be drawn from this trial, and no effect estimate for them is reported here. Readers wanting the numerical findings for these measured-but-not-formally-tested outcomes should consult the full scientific summary of results.

## Side effects

The trial systematically recorded serious side effects, side effects that led people to stop treatment permanently, and certain side effects of special interest that were defined in advance. It did not record every side effect that occurred, so the figures below do not represent a complete list of all side effects experienced.

**Serious side effects (any):** occurred in 33.4% of people on semaglutide compared with 36.4% on placebo.
- Heart-related serious side effects: 11.5% (semaglutide) vs 13.5% (placebo)
- Infections: 7.1% vs 8.4%
- Nervous system: 5.0% vs 5.6%
- Surgical/medical procedures: 4.9% vs 6.2%
- Cancers (neoplasms): 4.6% vs 4.6%
- Digestive system: 3.9% vs 3.7%

**Permanently stopping treatment because of a side effect:** occurred in 16.6% of people on semaglutide compared with 8.2% on placebo. Digestive system problems were the most common reason for stopping (10.0% vs 2.0%), followed by nervous system problems (1.4% vs 1.0%), metabolism/nutrition problems (1.2% vs 0.3%), and general/injection-site conditions (1.2% vs 0.5%). Stopping due to cancers (0.9% vs 1.2%) and infections (0.9% vs 1.0%) was similar or slightly lower with semaglutide.

**Specific side effects tracked in advance:**
- COVID-19 related events: 23.9% vs 24.4%
- Cancers: 4.8% vs 4.7%
- Gallbladder problems: 2.8% vs 2.3%
- Sudden kidney injury: 1.9% vs 2.3%
- Confirmed acute pancreatitis: 0.2% vs 0.3%

## Overall

In this trial, semaglutide reduced the combined occurrence of cardiovascular death, heart attack, and stroke compared with placebo, in people with overweight or obesity and established heart disease who did not have diabetes. Death from cardiovascular causes alone was numerically lower with semaglutide, but this difference was not reliable enough to be counted as a confirmed finding under the trial's pre-agreed testing plan; because of this, later outcomes in the testing sequence (the combined heart failure outcome and death from any cause) were not formally tested and do not support a conclusion about benefit from this trial. More people stopped semaglutide permanently because of side effects than stopped placebo, mainly due to digestive system problems.
