# Plain Language Summary: PARAGON-HF Trial

**What was this trial about?**

This trial studied people with heart failure whose heart's pumping chamber (the left ventricle) still pumps out a normal or near-normal proportion of blood with each beat — a condition called heart failure with preserved ejection fraction. The trial compared two medicines: sacubitril-valsartan (a combination medicine) against valsartan alone (a standard blood pressure medicine also used in heart failure). Neither the participants nor the study doctors knew who was taking which medicine.

4,822 people were randomly assigned to one of the two medicines. Due to a serious rule violation at one study site, that site was excluded, leaving 4,796 people whose results were analysed: 2,407 on sacubitril-valsartan and 2,389 on valsartan. Participants were followed for a typical period of around 35 months (with most followed between 30 and 41 months). The average age was around 73 years, and just over half of participants were women.

**Main result: hospital admissions for heart failure and cardiovascular deaths**

The main question the trial was designed to answer was whether sacubitril-valsartan would reduce the combined total number of hospital admissions for heart failure and deaths from heart or blood vessel-related causes, counted over the whole follow-up period (so a person could contribute more than one hospital admission).

- Sacubitril-valsartan group: 894 such events among 526 people
- Valsartan group: 1,009 such events among 557 people

The rate of events was somewhat lower with sacubitril-valsartan than with valsartan. However, this trial used a strict statistical rule for deciding whether a result counted as reliable, and the result for this main outcome did not meet that rule. This means the difference between the two medicines on this outcome cannot be counted as a reliably shown effect — it is uncertain whether the observed lower rate reflects a true difference between the treatments or could be due to chance.

**Kidney-related outcome**

The trial also planned to look at a combined kidney-related outcome (a large drop in kidney filtering function, kidney failure requiring treatment, or death from kidney disease). However, the trial's analysis plan specified that this outcome would only be formally, confirmatively tested if the main outcome above had met the reliability rule. Because the main outcome did not meet that rule, this kidney-related outcome was not formally tested according to the plan.

This outcome was measured, and events were recorded in both groups, but because it was not formally tested, no comparison of the two treatments on this outcome can be reported here, and it does not support a conclusion about whether one treatment was better than the other for kidney-related outcomes. Readers wanting the recorded numbers and their interpretation should refer to the full scientific summary of results.

**Stopping treatment**

- Sacubitril-valsartan group: 610 people (25.3%) stopped taking the study medicine for reasons other than death; 370 people (15.4%) stopped because of a side effect.
- Valsartan group: 638 people (26.7%) stopped for reasons other than death; 387 people (16.2%) stopped because of a side effect.

Before random assignment, all participants first took each medicine in a short trial run: 541 people stopped during the valsartan run-in period (340 due to a side effect), and 384 people stopped during the sacubitril-valsartan run-in period (262 due to a side effect). These people did not go on to the main randomised comparison.

**Side effects specifically monitored**

The trial specifically tracked certain side effects known to be linked to this type of medicine. This is not a list of all side effects experienced in the trial, only these particular ones:

| Side effect | Sacubitril-valsartan | Valsartan |
|---|---|---|
| Low blood pressure (systolic below 100 mmHg) | 380 people (15.8%) | 257 people (10.8%) |
| Raised creatinine (kidney marker), 2.0 mg/dl or higher | 261 people (10.8%) | 328 people (13.7%) |
| Raised creatinine, 2.5 mg/dl or higher | 97 people (4.0%) | 109 people (4.6%) |
| Raised creatinine, 3.0 mg/dl or higher | 38 people (1.6%) | 40 people (1.7%) |
| High potassium, above 5.5 mmol/litre | 316 people (13.2%) | 361 people (15.3%) |
| High potassium, above 6.0 mmol/litre | 75 people (3.1%) | 101 people (4.3%) |
| Angioedema (swelling under the skin, confirmed by expert review) | 14 people (0.6%) | 4 people (0.2%) — no one had airway blockage |
| Liver-related side effects | 151 people (6.3%) | 178 people (7.5%) |

Note: potassium results were based on slightly smaller numbers of people who had a potassium test (2,386 on sacubitril-valsartan; 2,367 on valsartan).

Overall rates of all side effects and serious side effects (not limited to the ones listed above) are not included in this summary, as they were not part of the data available for this report.
