# Summary of Trial Results: SYNTH-001

## What was this study about?

This study looked at people with type 2 diabetes who also had existing heart or blood vessel disease. It compared a medicine called synthemide (taken as a 15 mg tablet once a day) with a placebo (a dummy treatment with no active medicine). Neither the participants nor the study staff knew who received which treatment (this is called "double-blind"). People were assigned by chance (randomised) to one group or the other.

2,400 people took synthemide, and 2,398 people took the placebo.

## What were the main results?

**Heart and blood vessel problems (main outcome measured over 104 weeks, about 2 years)**

The study measured how many people had a serious event: death from cardiovascular (heart or blood vessel) causes, a non-fatal heart attack, or a non-fatal stroke. This combined measure is known as "MACE."

- People taking synthemide had a similar rate of these events compared to people taking placebo.
- The result (hazard ratio 0.91, meaning a 9% lower rate in the synthemide group) was not considered statistically significant, meaning it is not possible to be confident this difference was due to the drug rather than chance (95% confidence interval: 0.79 to 1.05; p-value = 0.19).
- This result was adjusted to account for multiple comparisons made during the study.

**Blood sugar control (a secondary measure, over 52 weeks, about 1 year)**

The study also measured HbA1c, a blood test that reflects average blood sugar levels over recent months.

- People taking synthemide had a larger drop in HbA1c compared to those taking placebo: a difference of 0.68 percentage points.
- This result was statistically significant, meaning it is unlikely to be due to chance alone (95% confidence interval: -0.81 to -0.55; p-value = 0.0001).
- This result was **not** adjusted for multiple comparisons.

## What side effects were reported?

The following side effects were reported in each group:

| Side effect | Synthemide group | Placebo group |
|---|---|---|
| Nausea (feeling sick) | 22.4% | 9.1% |
| Hypoglycaemia (low blood sugar) | 4.8% | 3.9% |
| Acute pancreatitis (sudden inflammation of the pancreas) | 0.4% | 0.2% |

## What does this mean?

In this study, synthemide lowered blood sugar levels (HbA1c) more than placebo, and this difference was statistically significant. However, for the main outcome — reducing serious heart and blood vessel events — there was no significant difference between synthemide and placebo. Side effects, including nausea, low blood sugar, and pancreatitis, were reported more often in people taking synthemide than in those taking placebo.
